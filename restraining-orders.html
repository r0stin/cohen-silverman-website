Restraining Orders

Protection, backed by a court order.

When someone won't keep their distance, a restraining or protection order sets enforceable limits. We prepare and argue your application, including urgent ones.

Book a consultation Call (905) 604-7662Call us

A restraining order (in some places called a protection order or peace bond) is a court order that can prohibit someone from contacting you, coming near you, or going to your home, work or school. Breaching it can carry serious consequences.

Applications are won on evidence. We help you assemble a clear, credible record, prepare your sworn statement and represent you at the hearing. When the risk is urgent, we can ask the court for a temporary order straight away.

Matters in this area are led by Rebecca Anderson and Noah Katz.

If you are in immediate danger, call 911 or your local emergency number. A court order is important, but it does not replace emergency help.

What we handle

Emergency and temporary orders

Urgent short-term protection while a full hearing is scheduled.

Partners and former partners

Protection from a current partner, an ex-partner or a family member.

Civil harassment orders

Protection from someone who lives nearby, an acquaintance, a coworker or a stranger.

Protecting your household

Orders that extend to children and other household members where appropriate.

Extensions and changes

Extending an order that is about to expire, or changing its terms.

Enforcing breaches

Taking action quickly when the other person violates the order.

How we approach it

  1. Assess safety

    We start with your immediate safety and whether an urgent order is needed.

  2. Build the evidence

    We organize messages, photos, police reports and witness accounts into a clear record.

  3. Apply to the court

    We prepare your application and sworn statement and file it in the right court.

  4. Argue the hearing

    We represent you in court, so you don't have to face the other person alone.

  5. Serve and enforce

    We make sure the order is served and act quickly if it's breached.

What to bring

  • A dated list of incidents, starting with the most serious
  • Threatening messages, voicemails and social media posts
  • Photos of injuries or damaged property
  • Police report numbers and officer names
  • Names and contact details of any witnesses
  • Copies of any previous court orders
